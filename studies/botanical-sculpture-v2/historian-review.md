# Botanical Garland: actual-mesh historical design review

Reviewed 2026-11-17 by an AI research-informed historical design reviewer. This is not a human historian's certification. Assessment concerns the actual mesh, compared with the preceding released mesh; no geometry was edited during review.

## Verdict

Request a small local iteration before accepting this sculptural revision. The new principal flowers have clearer broad petals and more readable cups. Their different outlines and positions preserve the diagonal composition and their connection to the surrounding acanthus. They avoid the rigid triangular pinwheel effect seen in an earlier unrelated trial.

## Changes worth making

1. Replace the regular crosshatched center disks. In the gold inspection close-ups they resemble knurled buttons with distinct rims, rather than modeled flower centers. Keep them low, reduce the separate disk-like edge, and merge sparse irregular connected seed/stamen relief into the bowl. A raised bulb or mathematically perfect dense spiral would not resolve the visual problem.
2. Soften selected doubled petal margins. The upper-left flower's lower petals and the lower-right flower's upper petal have raised outlines that can read as nested contours. Reduce one repeated lip where necessary while preserving the short curved overlap channels and unequal petal widths. Do not blur the entire flowers or remove their useful separation.

The rail and ground hierarchy remains coherent. No additional ornament or composition change is requested. The centers are the higher priority correction; the margin adjustment should remain conservative.

## Historical judgment

The combination of floral groups, tapering C/S scrolls, folded acanthus, shell centers and recessed grounds remains consistent with the French Louis XV design vocabulary informing this project. The [Met's frame attributed to Meissonnier, ca. 1735](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/209073), supports the sculptural frame language; the [Getty's French frame guide](https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/french_frames/LFF_Online_Gallery_Graphics.pdf) documents large flowers, stems and swept rails among Louis XV examples.

Those sources do not establish these particular five-petal flowers, center textures or complete composition as a documented period pattern. They are newly modeled ornaments within the intended vocabulary. The miniature photographic format and supported relief do not themselves invalidate the design's period reference; the present concern is whether the modeled forms read coherently as carving.

No numerical rating is assigned. Mechanical safety, printer performance and durability require separate checks and a physical prototype.
