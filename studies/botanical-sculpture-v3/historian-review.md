# Botanical Garland v3: actual-mesh historical design review

Reviewed 2026-11-17 by an AI research-informed historical design reviewer. No geometry was modified during this review. This assesses the modeled forms, not a physical print.

## Verdict

Accept v3 for a physical prototype, subject to separate mechanical and slicer checks. The two local requests from the v2 review are sufficiently resolved. No further essential historical or sculptural correction is identified before that test.

The regular crosshatched center disks no longer read as separate knurled buttons. Their softened edges and irregular low texture merge more naturally into the petal bowls. They remain visibly modeled centers, without returning to fragile stalks or a large raised bulb.

The selected duplicate petal margins are less emphatic. The flowers retain useful curved overlap channels, unequal broad petals and different outlines. Some margins still have strong contouring, especially the upper petal of the lower-right flower, but this now reads as a stylized fold rather than requiring another local revision.

The upper-left/lower-right diagonal composition, connection to acanthus, quieter grounds and continuous inner moulding remain coherent. The improvement is chiefly greater petal readability and better integration of the centers; it should not be described as a complete transformation into antique-quality carving.

## Remaining limitations

- Petals are comparatively broad and simplified. Their relief is more graphic than the generated carving study.
- Some transitions remain steep because this is continuous, supported 2.5D relief. The modeled folds should be judged from the actual-mesh images rather than assumed identical to the study.
- Very low center texture may soften further in a physical print. A prototype is more informative than another fine-detail revision at this point.

## Historical basis and claim

The floral groups, tapering C/S scrolls, folded acanthus, shell centers and recessed grounds remain consistent with the French Louis XV vocabulary used in this project. The [Met's frame attributed to Meissonnier, ca. 1735](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/209073), and the [Getty's French frame guide](https://www.getty.edu/art/exhibitions/french_frames/LFF_Online_Gallery_Graphics.pdf) support that broader carving language.

This is a new miniature design using that vocabulary. The particular five-petal flowers and their center textures are not claimed as an authenticated or documented period pattern. Modern manufacture does not itself invalidate the historical design reference. No numerical score or human certification is implied.
